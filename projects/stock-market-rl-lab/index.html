TL;DR

A PPO agent choosing between an ETF and cash lost to buy-and-hold after trading costs, and a pre-declared five-ETF replication came back adverse.

Problem #Link to section Problem

Can a PPO agent learn a useful long-or-cash allocation policy for a liquid equity ETF, compared with simple baselines, on unseen historical data after trading costs?

The comparison had to be fair: every baseline runs through the same environment as the agent, over the same dates, with the same costs and the same liquidation rule.

Data #Link to section Data

Daily SPY prices, with a test period from 2020-01-02 to 2025-12-31; for the replication, SPY, IWM, EFA, TLT and GLD, with cash earning the T-bill rate. Prices come from yfinance with their provenance recorded, and the exact series is identified by its SHA-256; the data is not redistributed. Splits are chronological, features strictly causal, and the scaler is fit on the training period only.

Approach #Link to section Approach

A Gymnasium environment with explicit cash and share accounting, next-open execution, commission and slippage in basis points, and terminal liquidation. PPO (and DQN as a second check) trained with Stable-Baselines3 over five seeds, against buy-and-hold, always-cash, a 50-day moving average and two random policies.

Then a pre-declared protocol: four PPO cells chosen on validation, five ETFs, a cheap comparator chosen on validation, a paired block bootstrap and a frozen three-way verdict rule, all recorded in an append-only ledger.

Result #Link to section Result

No, not with this setup. On SPY from 2020 to 2025, after 10 bps per one-way fill, the median of five PPO seeds returned less than buy-and-hold, with a lower Sharpe ratio and a smaller drawdown. Seed-to-seed variation was the dominant effect.

The five-ETF replication came back adverse under its frozen verdict rule. PPO's point estimate trailed buy-and-hold, though that 95% interval crosses zero; against the cheap comparator the whole interval is below zero. The repository also holds a classroom app and a web course that teach the project lesson by lesson.
